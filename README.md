# Predictors-of-Early-Childhood-Development-in-Nigeria-Using-Random-Forest-Models
Using R 
Random Forest modelling was used as a complementary predictive approach to classify developmental status among Nigerian children aged 24 to 59 months using the 2021 MICS and ECDI2030 indicator. Child, maternal, household and contextual characteristics were included as predictors. Model development involved training and test samples, grouped cross-validation and class weighting to address outcome imbalance. Performance was assessed using accuracy, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and other classification metrics. Permutation importance and partial dependence plots examined predictor contributions and predicted probabilities.

The model achieved 68.5% accuracy and an AUC of 0.726, with better identification of children who were not developmentally on track. Maternal education and household wealth were the strongest predictors, followed by geographical, ethnic and household characteristics. Findings complemented the primary survey-weighted logistic regression analysis and provided predictive, rather than causal or population-representative, estimates.

The findings highlight the importance of addressing socioeconomic inequalities, improving maternal education and expanding early learning opportunities to support early childhood development in Nigeria.
